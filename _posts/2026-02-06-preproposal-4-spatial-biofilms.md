# Pre-Proposal: Spatial Architecture of Multi-Kingdom Biofilms and Implications for Chronic Infections

## Background and Significance

Biofilms are structured microbial communities encased in self-produced extracellular matrix. They cause ~80% of chronic infections and exhibit extraordinary resistance to antibiotics and immune clearance. Most biofilm research focuses on single-species bacterial biofilms, yet clinical biofilms often contain bacteria, fungi, and other microorganisms. Multi-kingdom biofilms display emergent properties not predicted from single-species studies, including enhanced antibiotic resistance and altered virulence. Understanding the spatial architecture and inter-species interactions within multi-kingdom biofilms is critical for developing effective treatments for chronic infections.

## Knowledge Gap

Despite clinical importance, fundamental aspects of multi-kingdom biofilms remain unknown:

1. How are bacteria and fungi spatially organized within mixed biofilms?
2. Do specific bacterial-fungal spatial arrangements confer enhanced protection or virulence?
3. What molecular interactions govern spatial self-organization?
4. How does spatial structure affect antibiotic penetration and efficacy?
5. Can disrupting spatial organization sensitize biofilms to treatment?

## Proposed Research

### Specific Aims

**Aim 1: Map spatial architecture of bacterial-fungal biofilms at multiple scales**
- Use advanced microscopy to visualize mixed biofilms in 3D
- Employ spatial transcriptomics to map gene expression patterns
- Correlate structure with function (metabolic activity, antibiotic resistance)

**Aim 2: Identify molecular mechanisms governing spatial self-organization**
- Test roles of adhesins, matrix components, and secreted signals
- Use genetics to disrupt candidate organization mechanisms
- Determine if altered spatial structure affects biofilm properties

**Aim 3: Exploit spatial principles to enhance biofilm treatment efficacy**
- Design interventions that disrupt protective spatial arrangements
- Test combination strategies (spatial disruptors + antibiotics)
- Validate in clinically relevant infection models

## Approach and Methods

**Model Systems**: Focus on *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* + *Candida albicans* (chronic lung infections), *Staphylococcus aureus* + *C. albicans* (wound infections), and oral biofilm consortia.

**3D Biofilm Culture**: Use flow cells, microfluidic devices, and organoid co-cultures to grow biofilms under physiologically relevant conditions.

**Advanced Imaging**:
- **Confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM)**: 3D biofilm structure with fluorescent reporters
- **Light sheet microscopy**: Large-volume, high-resolution imaging
- **Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)**: Ultra-structural details
- **Correlative light and electron microscopy (CLEM)**: Link functional and structural data

**Spatial Transcriptomics**: 
- Slide-seq or Visium for spatial RNA-seq of biofilm sections
- MERFISH or seqFISH for highly multiplexed in situ gene expression
- Correlate spatial gene expression with biofilm architecture

**Quantitative Image Analysis**:
- Segment bacterial and fungal regions
- Measure spatial metrics: contact area, intermixing, local densities
- Develop computational models of spatial organization

**Mechanistic Studies**:
- Candidate approach: Test mutants lacking adhesins (bacterial Als proteins, fungal Flo proteins), matrix components, or QS systems
- Screen chemical libraries for compounds disrupting spatial organization
- Use synthetic biology to engineer spatial patterns

**Antibiotic Testing**:
- Measure antibiotic penetration using fluorescent antibiotics
- Correlate penetration with spatial structure
- Test if spatial disruption enhances antibiotic efficacy

**In Vivo Validation**:
- Murine wound infection model (*S. aureus* + *C. albicans*)
- Rat lung infection model (*P. aeruginosa* + *C. albicans*)
- Measure biofilm burden, tissue damage, immune responses
- Test spatial disruption + antibiotic combination therapy

## Expected Outcomes and Impact

This research will:
1. Provide first comprehensive spatial map of bacterial-fungal biofilms
2. Reveal principles governing multi-kingdom biofilm self-organization
3. Identify new therapeutic targets (spatial organization mechanisms)
4. Develop combination therapies with enhanced efficacy against chronic infections
5. Establish spatial analysis frameworks applicable to other microbiome contexts

Impact: Chronic infections cause immense morbidity and healthcare costs. New treatment strategies are urgently needed. Understanding and targeting spatial organization represents a fundamentally new approach to biofilm control.

## Feasibility

Our lab has expertise in biofilm biology and microbial imaging. We have established collaborations with spatial transcriptomics experts and access to required imaging facilities. Preliminary data show striking spatial segregation of *P. aeruginosa* and *C. albicans* in mixed biofilms, with fungi forming protective cores. This spatial arrangement correlates with increased antibiotic resistance, supporting our hypothesis.

## Translational Potential

Findings will inform treatment of:
- Chronic wound infections (diabetic foot ulcers, burns)
- Cystic fibrosis lung infections
- Catheter-associated infections
- Prosthetic joint infections
- Chronic otitis media

We will partner with clinicians and industry to develop clinical-grade spatial disruption agents and test in clinical trials.

## Broader Impacts

Spatial organization is a universal feature of microbial communities. Principles discovered here will apply to environmental biofilms, industrial biofouling, and tissue-associated microbiomes. The research will train students in cutting-edge imaging, spatial biology, and translational microbiology. We will develop open-source image analysis tools for the community.

## Conclusion

Multi-kingdom biofilms represent a frontier in infection biology. Spatial analysis technologies now enable unprecedented insight into these complex systems. This research will transform our understanding of chronic infections and generate new therapeutic strategies with significant clinical impact.
