# Pre-Proposal: Microbiome-Immune System Dialogue - How Bacterial-Fungal Communities Train Immunity

## Background and Significance

The immune system develops and maintains function through continuous dialogue with the microbiome. Most research focuses on bacterial effects on immunity, but the mycobiome (fungal microbiome) also modulates immune responses. Critically, bacteria and fungi are sensed by distinct immune receptors and elicit different responses. How does the immune system integrate signals from multi-kingdom microbiomes? Do bacterial-fungal consortia train immunity differently than bacteria alone? Understanding these interactions is essential for rationally designing microbiome-based immunotherapies and for the One Health initiative, where microbial exposures across environments shape immune function.

## Knowledge Gap

Despite extensive work on microbiome-immune interactions, key questions remain:

1. How do bacterial-fungal consortia versus mono-kingdom communities affect immune development and function?
2. What are the molecular mechanisms of cross-kingdom immune signaling integration?
3. Can synthetic multi-kingdom communities be designed to optimize immune training?
4. How do early-life exposures to bacterial-fungal communities influence lifelong immune function?
5. Can microbiome-based interventions prevent immune-mediated diseases (allergy, autoimmunity)?

## Proposed Research

### Specific Aims

**Aim 1: Define immune responses to bacterial-fungal consortia versus mono-kingdom communities**
- Colonize germ-free mice with bacteria-only, fungi-only, or bacterial-fungal consortia
- Comprehensively profile immune system development and function
- Identify unique immunological features of multi-kingdom colonization

**Aim 2: Determine molecular mechanisms of cross-kingdom immune signal integration**
- Use receptor knockout mice to dissect signaling pathways
- Employ spatial analysis to map where and when cross-kingdom sensing occurs
- Define transcriptional programs induced by bacterial-fungal communities

**Aim 3: Design synthetic communities that optimally train immunity**
- Test combinations of bacteria and fungi with varying immunological properties
- Optimize for protective immunity without excessive inflammation
- Validate in disease models (infection challenge, allergy, colitis)

## Approach and Methods

**Gnotobiotic Mouse Models**:
- Germ-free mice colonized with defined communities
- **Bacteria-only**: Altered Schaedler Flora (ASF) or custom synthetic community
- **Fungi-only**: *Candida albicans*, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, representative gut fungi
- **Bacterial-fungal**: Combinations of above
- **Controls**: Germ-free, conventionally raised

**Immune Profiling**:
- **Cellular immunity**: Flow cytometry of lymphoid organs, gut lamina propria (T cell subsets, B cells, ILCs, myeloid cells)
- **Serum antibodies**: Isotype profiling (IgA, IgG subclasses, IgE)
- **Cytokines**: Multiplex assays for serum and tissue cytokines
- **Transcriptomics**: RNA-seq of intestinal epithelium, immune cells, lymphoid organs
- **Metabolomics**: Measure microbial metabolites affecting immunity (SCFAs, tryptophan metabolites)

**Temporal Dynamics**:
- Colonize at birth or weaning
- Longitudinal sampling (weekly for 8-12 weeks)
- Define critical windows for immune training

**Spatial Analysis**:
- Image gut tissues with immunofluorescence and spatial transcriptomics
- Map where bacteria, fungi, and immune cells interact
- Correlate spatial organization with immune phenotypes

**Mechanistic Studies**:
- **Pattern recognition receptors**: Use TLR, CLR, and NOD-like receptor knockout mice
- **Immune cell depletion**: Deplete specific populations (ILCs, Tregs, etc.) to test necessity
- **Bacterial/fungal mutants**: Use strains deficient in specific immunomodulatory molecules (flagellin, β-glucan, etc.)

**Functional Challenges**:
- **Infection**: Challenge with pathogens (*Salmonella*, *Listeria*, *Aspergillus*); measure protection
- **Allergy**: Ovalbumin sensitization and challenge; measure airway inflammation
- **Autoimmunity**: Dextran sodium sulfate (DSS) colitis; measure disease severity
- **Metabolic**: High-fat diet; measure glucose tolerance, inflammation

**Community Design and Optimization**:
- Test combinations of bacterial and fungal strains
- Vary ratios, timing of colonization, community complexity
- Use machine learning to predict optimal combinations
- Iteratively test predictions

**Translation to Humans**:
- Analyze immune profiles in human cohorts with varying mycobiome compositions
- Test if findings from mouse studies predict human immune phenotypes
- Design early-life intervention trials (pregnant mothers, infants)

## Expected Outcomes and Impact

This research will:
1. Reveal how multi-kingdom microbiomes uniquely shape immunity
2. Identify molecular mechanisms of cross-kingdom immune signal integration
3. Generate rationally designed communities for immune training
4. Define critical windows and microbial compositions for optimal immune development
5. Provide foundation for microbiome-based prevention of immune-mediated diseases

Impact: Immune-mediated diseases (allergy, asthma, autoimmunity) are epidemic in developed countries, linked to reduced microbial exposures. This work could enable interventions to restore appropriate immune training, potentially preventing these conditions. It also has implications for vaccination responses, cancer immunotherapy, and infectious disease susceptibility.

## Innovation

- First systematic comparison of immune responses to bacterial-only vs. multi-kingdom microbiomes
- Integration of spatial, temporal, and mechanistic approaches
- Rational design of immunomodulatory communities based on mechanistic understanding
- Translation to human immune health and disease prevention
- Addresses One Health concept by examining how environmental microbial exposures shape immunity

## Feasibility

Our team includes immunologists, microbiologists, and computational biologists. We have established gnotobiotic facilities and fungal strain collections. Preliminary data show that mice colonized with bacteria + *C. albicans* develop enhanced Th17 responses compared to bacteria-only controls, and are better protected against *Salmonella* challenge. This supports our hypothesis that multi-kingdom communities provide superior immune training.

Collaborations with clinical research groups provide access to human cohorts. The project builds on foundational work from multiple fields and is positioned to make significant advances.

## Broader Impacts

This work addresses fundamental questions about immune development with clear translational potential. Beyond direct health applications, it will:
- Train next-generation scientists in integrative host-microbe biology
- Develop open-source protocols for gnotobiotic multi-kingdom studies
- Contribute to One Health framework by revealing immune consequences of environmental microbial exposures
- Inform agricultural practices (livestock microbiome and immunity)
- Advance personalized medicine (tailoring microbial exposures to individual immune profiles)

## Clinical Translation Path

We will:
- Partner with probiotic/synbiotic companies to develop multi-kingdom formulations
- Conduct safety and tolerability studies
- Design randomized controlled trials in high-risk populations (C-section delivered infants, antibiotic-exposed infants)
- Measure immune outcomes and disease incidence
- Work with regulatory agencies to establish approval pathways for multi-kingdom live biotherapeutics

## Conclusion

The immune system evolved to interact with complex multi-kingdom microbiomes, yet most microbiome research and therapeutics focus solely on bacteria. Understanding and harnessing bacterial-fungal immune training could unlock new strategies for preventing immune-mediated diseases and enhancing protective immunity.
