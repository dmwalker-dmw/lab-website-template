# Pre-Proposal: One Microbiome Initiative - Tracking Antimicrobial Resistance Across Human-Animal-Environment Interfaces

## Background and Significance

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is a global health crisis, projected to cause 10 million deaths annually by 2050. AMR genes flow between human, animal, and environmental microbiomes through complex transmission networks. The One Health framework recognizes these interconnections but has not been fully extended to microbiomes. The "One Microbiome" concept proposes that human, animal, and environmental microbiomes form an integrated system requiring coordinated surveillance and management. Understanding AMR transmission across these interfaces is essential for effective interventions, yet comprehensive tracking systems are lacking.

## Knowledge Gap

Current AMR surveillance is fragmented, focusing on clinical isolates or specific environmental samples. Critical gaps include:

1. What are the major pathways for AMR gene flow between human, animal, and environmental microbiomes?
2. Which interfaces (livestock-farm worker, wastewater-surface water, pet-owner) pose highest transmission risks?
3. How do agricultural practices (antibiotic use in livestock) affect human microbiomes?
4. Can environmental reservoirs be managed to reduce AMR transmission?
5. What integrated surveillance strategies can detect emerging resistance early?

## Proposed Research

### Specific Aims

**Aim 1: Map AMR gene flow across One Microbiome continuum**
- Conduct coordinated sampling of human, animal, and environmental microbiomes at multiple sites
- Use metagenomics to track AMR gene prevalence and mobility
- Identify high-risk transmission interfaces

**Aim 2: Quantify AMR transmission dynamics at critical interfaces**
- Longitudinal sampling of paired populations (farm workers-livestock, pet owners-pets)
- Source tracking to determine transmission directionality
- Model transmission rates and identify risk factors

**Aim 3: Develop and test intervention strategies**
- Design interventions targeting high-risk interfaces
- Test environmental management strategies (wastewater treatment optimization)
- Pilot integrated surveillance system

## Approach and Methods

**Study Sites**: Establish 3-5 field sites representing different One Health contexts:
- Livestock farms (dairy, poultry)
- Companion animal hospitals
- Wastewater treatment plants
- Urban parks with wildlife-human contact
- Coastal environments (aquaculture-marine ecosystem interface)

**Sample Collection**:
- **Human**: Stool samples from workers/community members (n=100-200 per site)
- **Animal**: Fecal/environmental samples from livestock, pets, wildlife
- **Environment**: Soil, water, air samples from relevant locations
- **Longitudinal**: Monthly sampling for 2 years

**Metagenomic Analysis**:
- Shotgun metagenomics for all samples
- Target AMR genes from CARD database
- Characterize mobile genetic elements (plasmids, transposons)
- Track taxonomic context (which bacteria carry which resistance genes)

**AMR Gene Tracking**:
- Build database of AMR gene variants
- Use sequence similarity networks to track gene flow
- Phylogenetic analysis to determine transmission events vs. convergent evolution

**Source Tracking**:
- SourceTracker or FEAST algorithms to determine microbiome mixing
- Human-associated vs. animal-associated vs. environment-associated bacterial markers
- Directional transmission modeling

**Risk Factor Analysis**:
- Questionnaires on antibiotic use, hygiene practices, contact patterns
- Statistical modeling (mixed-effects models, machine learning) to identify risk factors
- Quantify transmission rates at different interfaces

**Intervention Testing**:
- **Environmental**: Enhanced wastewater treatment, composting protocols
- **Behavioral**: Hand hygiene, protective equipment use
- **Policy**: Reduced agricultural antibiotic use
- Before-after and/or cluster-randomized trial designs

**Integrated Surveillance Platform**:
- Develop standardized sampling and analysis protocols
- Create data integration and visualization dashboard
- Real-time alerts for novel or high-risk resistance genes
- Make data publicly available (with appropriate privacy protections)

## Expected Outcomes and Impact

This research will:
1. Generate first comprehensive map of AMR flow across One Microbiome continuum
2. Identify highest-risk transmission interfaces requiring intervention
3. Quantify impacts of agricultural antibiotic use on human microbiomes
4. Develop evidence-based intervention strategies
5. Establish model for integrated One Microbiome surveillance

Impact: This work directly addresses the AMR crisis by revealing transmission pathways and enabling targeted interventions. It operationalizes the One Microbiome concept with practical surveillance and management strategies. Findings will inform agricultural policy, environmental management, and public health guidelines.

## Innovation

- First large-scale, integrated study of AMR across complete One Health continuum
- Longitudinal design enabling transmission dynamics quantification
- Integration of genomic, epidemiological, and ecological approaches
- Development of translatable surveillance infrastructure
- Direct testing of intervention strategies
- Open data platform for global One Microbiome research

## Feasibility

Our team includes experts in microbial ecology, epidemiology, veterinary medicine, and environmental science. We have established relationships with farm cooperatives, veterinary hospitals, and wastewater utilities. Preliminary metagenomic data from farm environments show high AMR gene prevalence and mobility. Computational pipelines for AMR gene detection are established. Funding partnerships with USDA, EPA, and CDC are in development.

## Multi-Disciplinary Approach

This project requires integration across disciplines:
- **Microbial ecology**: Community dynamics, transmission
- **Epidemiology**: Risk factors, intervention studies
- **Veterinary medicine**: Animal microbiomes, zoonotic risks
- **Environmental science**: Environmental reservoirs, management strategies
- **Data science**: Metagenomics, network analysis, visualization
- **Policy**: Translation to regulations and guidelines

## Broader Impacts

Beyond AMR, this framework applies to:
- Zoonotic pathogen surveillance (pandemic preparedness)
- Beneficial microbiome preservation
- Environmental microbiome health assessment
- Agricultural sustainability

Training: We will train graduate students and postdocs in One Health approaches, preparing next-generation scientists for complex global health challenges. Community engagement will include farmer workshops, public science communication, and policy briefings.

## Conclusion

AMR threatens modern medicine. Understanding and interrupting transmission requires a One Microbiome perspective. This research will generate foundational knowledge, practical tools, and policy-relevant insights to address this global crisis.
