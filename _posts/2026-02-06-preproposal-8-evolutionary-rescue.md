# Pre-Proposal: Evolutionary Rescue in Dysbiotic Microbiomes - Can Beneficial Strains Evolve Fast Enough?

## Background and Significance

Microbiome dysbiosis involves loss of beneficial species and expansion of potential pathogens. Restoration attempts through probiotics or FMT often fail because introduced beneficial strains cannot establish in the altered community. However, microbes evolve rapidly—on timescales of days to weeks in laboratory settings. "Evolutionary rescue" occurs when populations avoid extinction through rapid adaptive evolution. Could beneficial commensals evolve to establish in dysbiotic communities? Understanding and accelerating this process could improve microbiome restoration therapies. This question bridges ecology and evolution, areas where the Sanchez, Estrela, and Gore laboratories have made fundamental contributions.

## Knowledge Gap

The role of evolution in microbiome restoration is virtually unexplored:

1. Do beneficial bacteria evolve when introduced into dysbiotic communities?
2. What adaptations enable establishment in hostile environments?
3. Can we predict which strains have evolutionary potential for rescue?
4. Does evolution occur fast enough to be clinically relevant?
5. Can we pre-adapt strains to increase restoration success?

## Proposed Research

### Specific Aims

**Aim 1: Test whether beneficial strains undergo evolutionary rescue in dysbiotic communities**
- Create dysbiotic synthetic communities (depleted of beneficial species)
- Introduce beneficial strains and track evolutionary dynamics
- Whole-genome sequencing to identify adaptive mutations
- Test if evolved strains establish better than ancestors

**Aim 2: Identify genetic and ecological predictors of evolutionary rescue potential**
- Screen diverse beneficial strains for rescue capability
- Correlate success with genetic features (genome size, mobile elements, regulatory flexibility)
- Test if pre-adaptation in dysbiotic conditions improves outcomes

**Aim 3: Translate evolutionary principles to enhance microbiome restoration therapies**
- Pre-adapt probiotic strains for dysbiotic environments
- Test evolved strains in gnotobiotic mouse models
- Pilot clinical trial with evolution-enhanced probiotics

## Approach and Methods

**Dysbiotic Community Models**:
- Create synthetic communities representing dysbiotic states
- Include pathobionts (*Enterobacteriaceae*, *Enterococcus*)
- Exclude typical beneficial species (*Faecalibacterium*, *Akkermansia*)
- Model post-antibiotic dysbiosis or IBD-associated dysbiosis

**Evolutionary Rescue Experiments**:
- Introduce beneficial strains at low abundance
- Serial passage communities (batch culture or chemostats)
- Track strain abundance by qPCR or sequencing
- Identify successful establishment vs. extinction

**Genome Sequencing**:
- Whole-genome sequencing of ancestral and evolved populations
- Identify mutations under selection
- Parallel evolution analysis (do same mutations arise repeatedly?)
- Functional validation of candidate adaptive mutations

**Mechanistic Studies**:
- Test if key mutations alter:
  - Nutrient utilization (accessing alternative carbon sources)
  - Stress resistance (pH, bile acids, antimicrobials)
  - Competition (antagonistic molecules, resource competition)
  - Cooperation (cross-feeding with other community members)

**Predictive Modeling**:
- Use machine learning to predict rescue potential from strain genetic features
- Test predictions prospectively on novel strains
- Develop guidelines for selecting strains with high rescue potential

**Pre-Adaptation**:
- Evolve beneficial strains in dysbiotic community conditions before introduction
- Test if pre-adapted strains establish more effectively
- Optimize pre-adaptation protocols (duration, selection pressure)

**In Vivo Validation**:
- Gnotobiotic mice with dysbiotic communities
- Introduce ancestral vs. evolved beneficial strains
- Measure engraftment, community restoration, host health markers
- Test in antibiotic-pretreated model

**Clinical Translation**:
- Collaborate with probiotic manufacturers to evolve commercial strains
- Safety and stability testing of evolved strains
- Phase I/II clinical trial comparing standard vs. evolution-enhanced probiotics

## Expected Outcomes and Impact

This research will:
1. Establish that evolutionary rescue occurs in microbiomes and can be harnessed therapeutically
2. Identify genetic features predicting evolutionary rescue potential
3. Develop pre-adaptation protocols to enhance probiotic efficacy
4. Generate evolved probiotic strains with improved establishment in dysbiotic conditions
5. Demonstrate clinical feasibility of evolution-enhanced microbiome restoration

Impact: Probiotic failure is a major limitation of current microbiome therapeutics. Evolution-based approaches could dramatically improve success rates. This represents a new paradigm: using evolution as a tool rather than viewing it as a complication.

## Feasibility

Our lab has expertise in experimental evolution and synthetic communities. Preliminary data show that *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii* (major beneficial commensal) fails to establish in dysbiotic synthetic communities but evolves variants that can persist after 30 days of serial passage. Sequencing reveals mutations in carbohydrate utilization genes. We have established collaborations with the Estrela lab (evolution of cooperation) and probiotic companies interested in strain optimization.

## Broader Impacts

Beyond direct therapeutic applications, this work will:
- Establish general principles of evolutionary rescue in multi-species communities
- Inform conservation biology (rescuing endangered species in degraded environments)
- Advance understanding of adaptation in complex ecological contexts
- Train students in evolutionary ecology and translational research

## Regulatory Considerations

Evolved strains will require safety assessment. We will:
- Sequence evolved strains to verify no acquisition of virulence or resistance genes
- Test antibiotic susceptibility
- Assess virulence in cell culture and animal models
- Follow FDA guidance for live biotherapeutic products

## Conclusion

Evolution is fundamental to biology but underutilized in microbiome therapeutics. Harnessing evolutionary rescue could transform microbiome restoration from an unreliable endeavor to a predictable, engineerable process with significant clinical impact.
