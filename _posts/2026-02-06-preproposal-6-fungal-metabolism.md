# Pre-Proposal: Metabolic Basis of Fungal Pathogenesis in Complex Gut Microbiomes

## Background and Significance

*Candida albicans* asymptomatically colonizes the gut of most humans but can become pathogenic under certain conditions, causing mucosal infections or life-threatening invasive candidiasis. The transition from commensal to pathogen is poorly understood but appears to involve interactions with the bacterial microbiome and host. Recent evidence suggests metabolic competition and cross-feeding between bacteria and *Candida* regulate fungal virulence, morphology, and colonization levels. However, these interactions have been studied primarily in simplified systems. Understanding how *Candida* metabolism is constrained and regulated within complex bacterial communities could reveal novel therapeutic targets.

## Knowledge Gap

Despite clinical importance, fundamental questions about fungal-bacterial metabolic interactions remain:

1. How does the bacterial microbiome shape the metabolic environment experienced by gut fungi?
2. Which bacterial metabolites directly affect fungal virulence and morphology?
3. Does *Candida* alter its metabolism when transitioning from commensal to pathogen?
4. Can bacterial metabolic interventions prevent pathogenic transitions?
5. How do host-derived nutrients vs. bacteria-derived metabolites affect fungal behavior?

## Proposed Research

### Specific Aims

**Aim 1: Define metabolic landscape of bacterial-fungal gut communities**
- Use synthetic communities + *Candida* with varying pathogenic potential
- Measure metabolomes of communities over time
- Correlate metabolic profiles with fungal virulence phenotypes

**Aim 2: Identify bacterial metabolites regulating fungal pathogenesis**
- Test candidate metabolites (short-chain fatty acids, amino acid derivatives, etc.)
- Measure effects on hyphal formation, biofilm, virulence gene expression
- Define molecular mechanisms using fungal genetics and transcriptomics

**Aim 3: Exploit metabolic dependencies for therapeutic intervention**
- Engineer bacteria to overproduce inhibitory metabolites
- Test probiotic/synbiotic interventions in gnotobiotic models
- Assess efficacy and safety for clinical translation

## Approach and Methods

**Synthetic Communities**: 
- Build defined communities (10-20 strains) representing major gut bacterial phyla
- Include known butyrate producers (*Faecalibacterium*, *Roseburia*)
- Include species known to interact with *Candida* (*Bacteroides*, *Lactobacillus*)

**Candida Strains**:
- Clinical isolates with varying virulence
- Mutants in virulence pathways (hyphal formation, adhesion)
- Fluorescent reporters for metabolic state and virulence gene expression

**Culture Systems**:
- Batch cultures in chemically defined media
- Continuous cultures (chemostats) for steady-state analysis
- Microfluidic devices for spatial heterogeneity

**Metabolomics**:
- Untargeted LC-MS/MS to profile global metabolome
- Targeted quantification of candidate molecules
- Time-series sampling to capture metabolic dynamics
- Spatial metabolomics in microfluidic systems

**Fungal Phenotyping**:
- **Morphology**: Yeast-to-hyphal transition (key virulence determinant)
- **Biofilm formation**: Crystal violet assay, confocal microscopy
- **Gene expression**: RNA-seq and RT-qPCR for virulence genes
- **Stress resistance**: Growth under immune-relevant stresses (oxidative, nitrosative)

**Mechanistic Studies**:
- **Candidate metabolite testing**: Add purified metabolites to *Candida* cultures; measure phenotypes
- **Dose-response**: Define effective concentrations
- **Transcriptomics**: RNA-seq to identify regulated pathways
- **Genetics**: Use *Candida* mutant libraries to identify metabolite-responsive pathways

**Bacterial Engineering**:
- Use synthetic biology to increase production of inhibitory metabolites
- CRISPR-based enhancement of biosynthetic pathways
- Test engineered strains in synthetic communities

**In Vivo Validation**:
- Gnotobiotic mice colonized with synthetic communities ± engineered strains
- Challenge with virulent *Candida* (oral gavage or IV for systemic infection)
- Measure fungal burden, tissue invasion, inflammation, survival
- Test in antibiotic-treated model (mimics clinical risk factor)

**Clinical Relevance**:
- Analyze metabolomes from human stool samples with high vs. low *Candida* burdens
- Validate that metabolites identified in synthetic systems are relevant in vivo

## Expected Outcomes and Impact

This research will:
1. Map metabolic interactions between gut bacteria and *Candida*
2. Identify bacterial metabolites that prevent pathogenic transitions
3. Define molecular mechanisms of metabolite-mediated virulence regulation
4. Develop engineered probiotic strains that produce anti-virulence metabolites
5. Provide rational basis for metabolic interventions (diet, prebiotics, probiotics)

Impact: *Candida* infections have limited treatment options and rising antifungal resistance. Microbiome-based strategies that prevent pathogenesis without killing the fungus (reducing selection for resistance) represent a paradigm shift. This could benefit immunocompromised patients, ICU patients, and others at high risk.

## Innovation

- First systematic mapping of bacterial metabolic effects on fungal virulence in complex communities
- Integration of community ecology, metabolomics, and fungal molecular biology
- Synthetic biology approach to engineer therapeutic bacteria
- Translational focus with clear path to clinical application
- Addresses both invasive infections and mucosal candidiasis

## Feasibility

Our laboratory has established expertise in bacterial-fungal co-culture and metabolomics. We have extensive *Candida* strain and mutant collections. Preliminary data show that *Candida* hyphal formation is suppressed in co-culture with *Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron*, and metabolomics reveals accumulation of specific amino acid metabolites. Collaborations with Sanchez and Estrela labs will provide expertise in metabolic modeling. Gnotobiotic facilities and clinical partnerships are established.

## Translational Pathway

We will optimize engineered strains for manufacturability and stability. Safety testing will include assessment in immunocompromised animal models. Regulatory pathway will follow live biotherapeutic product guidelines. Target indications: prevention of invasive candidiasis in high-risk hospitalized patients, treatment of recurrent vulvovaginal candidiasis.

Industry partnerships are in development for scale-up and clinical trials.

## Broader Impacts

Metabolic interactions are fundamental to microbiome function. Principles discovered here will apply to other fungal pathogens (*Aspergillus*, *Cryptococcus*) and to understanding how microbiomes regulate other phenotypes (inflammation, metabolism). The work exemplifies integrative, mechanistic microbiome science with clear translational potential.

## Conclusion

Understanding and manipulating bacterial-fungal metabolic interactions could transform prevention and treatment of fungal infections. This research combines cutting-edge tools from microbial ecology, metabolomics, and synthetic biology to address a critical gap in fungal pathogenesis.
