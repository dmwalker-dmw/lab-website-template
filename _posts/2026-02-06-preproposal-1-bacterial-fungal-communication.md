# Pre-Proposal: Deciphering Bacterial-Fungal Cross-Kingdom Communication Networks in the Human Gut

## Background and Significance

The human gut microbiome comprises diverse bacterial and fungal species that coexist and interact in complex ways. While bacterial communication via quorum sensing is well-characterized, bacterial-fungal cross-kingdom communication remains poorly understood. This gap is critical because fungi like *Candida albicans* colonize most humans, and bacterial-fungal interactions can influence susceptibility to invasive fungal infections, inflammatory bowel disease, and other conditions. Recent evidence suggests bacteria and fungi communicate through small molecules, but the molecular mechanisms, ecological context, and functional consequences remain largely unknown.

## Knowledge Gap

Current research has focused primarily on bacterial communities or fungal communities in isolation. The few studies of bacterial-fungal interactions have been limited to pairwise combinations in laboratory conditions. Critical unknowns include:

1. What is the full repertoire of signaling molecules mediating bacterial-fungal communication in complex communities?
2. How do bacterial signals affect fungal gene expression, morphology, and virulence?
3. How do fungal signals modulate bacterial community structure and function?
4. What is the spatial organization of bacterial-fungal communication networks in the gut environment?
5. How do these communication networks influence host immune responses and disease susceptibility?

## Proposed Research

### Specific Aims

**Aim 1: Map the chemical communication network between gut bacteria and fungi**
- Use synthetic community approaches to systematically screen bacterial-fungal pairs
- Employ metabolomics to identify signaling molecules
- Validate candidate signals using defined synthetic communities

**Aim 2: Characterize functional consequences of cross-kingdom signaling**
- Measure effects of bacterial signals on fungal morphology, biofilm formation, and virulence factor expression
- Assess impacts of fungal signals on bacterial gene expression and community composition
- Use transcriptomics and proteomics to define molecular responses

**Aim 3: Define spatial organization and host interactions**
- Develop microfluidic gut-on-a-chip systems with bacterial-fungal communities
- Image bacterial-fungal biofilm architecture using confocal microscopy and spatial transcriptomics
- Assess host epithelial and immune cell responses to bacterial-fungal communities vs. mono-kingdom controls

## Approach and Methods

**Model Systems**: We will use a bottom-up synthetic community approach, beginning with well-characterized bacterial strains (*Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron*, *Escherichia coli*, *Lactobacillus* spp.) and fungal strains (*Candida albicans*, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*). Communities will be grown in chemically defined media mimicking gut conditions.

**Chemical Ecology**: Untargeted metabolomics (LC-MS/MS) will identify candidate signaling molecules. Synthetic versions of candidates will be tested for biological activity.

**Molecular Mechanisms**: RNA-seq and proteomics will define transcriptional and post-transcriptional responses. CRISPR-based genetic manipulation will validate mechanism.

**Spatial Analysis**: Microfluidic devices with integrated sensors will enable real-time monitoring of bacterial-fungal interactions under flow conditions. Spatial transcriptomics will map gene expression patterns.

**Computational Modeling**: Collaborations with theoretical ecologists will develop predictive models of communication network dynamics.

## Expected Outcomes and Impact

This research will:
1. Generate the first comprehensive map of bacterial-fungal communication molecules in gut-relevant conditions
2. Reveal how cross-kingdom signaling regulates community assembly and stability
3. Identify novel therapeutic targets for preventing fungal infections or treating dysbiosis
4. Establish experimental and computational frameworks for studying multi-kingdom microbiomes

The work directly addresses knowledge gaps in bacterial-fungal interactions and microbial communication identified in recent reviews. It will enable rational design of synthetic communities for therapeutic applications and provide foundational knowledge for the One Microbiome initiative by clarifying how different microbial kingdoms interact.

## Innovation

- First systematic mapping of bacterial-fungal communication networks using synthetic community approaches
- Integration of chemical ecology, systems biology, and spatial analysis in a unified framework
- Novel microfluidic platforms for studying multi-kingdom communities under physiologically relevant conditions
- Computational models that predict emergent properties of cross-kingdom interactions

## Timeline and Resources

**Year 1**: Establish synthetic communities, optimize culture conditions, perform metabolomics screens
**Year 2**: Validate candidate signals, characterize molecular mechanisms using omics approaches
**Year 3**: Develop and validate spatial analysis platforms, integrate data into predictive models

**Key Resources Needed**:
- LC-MS/MS metabolomics facility
- RNA-seq and proteomics capabilities
- Microfluidic device fabrication
- Computational infrastructure for multi-omics data integration
- Collaborative partnerships with theoretical ecologists and immunologists

## Feasibility

The PI has expertise in microbial ecology and synthetic communities. Preliminary data show that *C. albicans* morphology changes in the presence of spent medium from *B. thetaiotaomicron*, suggesting active cross-talk. Core facilities for metabolomics and genomics are available. Collaborations with leaders in the field (Gore, Sanchez, Friedman labs) will provide methodological expertise.

## Conclusion

Understanding bacterial-fungal communication is essential for comprehending microbiome function and dysfunction. This research will generate foundational knowledge with direct translational potential for treating fungal infections and microbiome-associated diseases.
